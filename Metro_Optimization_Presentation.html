The Research Problem

Urban mass transit systems operate at a severe financial deficit because they are historically designed around passenger convenience.

The BMRCL operates a fleet of 58 trains using static timetables. Running high-frequency schedules during off-peak hours results in half-empty trains and massive operational losses.

Research Question

What is the mathematically optimal, time-varying train dispatch schedule across the Namma Metro network that maximizes expected daily operational profit, without violating physical signaling constraints, rolling stock limits, or minimum public service mandates?

Motivation for Dataset Selection

To ground our Linear Program in reality, the core constraint must be passenger demand. We utilized secondary, empirical footfall data provided by BMRCL for the entire month of August 2025.

Why this dataset?

  • Complete Census: It acts as a true census of all smart-card and token taps across all 69 operational stations.
  • Capturing True Variance: By taking an entire month, we capture real-world variance including late-night stragglers and massive demand spikes (e.g., sporting events).
  • Probabilistic Parameters: Aggregating historical data allows us to transition from deterministic assumptions to calculating the expected value (mean) of a random variable representing footfall for any given hour on a typical business day.

Temporal Demand Analysis

Detailed Insight:

The time-series reveals severe temporal asymmetry. The system's demand is heavily bimodal.

  • Dead Zones: Footfall drops near zero between 01:00 and 04:00 Hrs. A static schedule wastes immense capital here.
  • Peak Rushes: Demand spikes aggressively during morning (08:00–10:00 Hrs) and evening (17:00–20:00 Hrs) commutes, hitting the system's capacity limits.

Spatial Variance & Line Disparity

Detailed Insight:

The boxplot demonstrates massive spatial variance between the two lines, mathematically invalidating a flat-rate schedule.

  • Purple Line Dominance: Exhibits a significantly higher median demand and a much wider Interquartile Range (IQR).
  • Extreme Outliers: The top whisker of the Purple Line highlights maximum peak outliers exceeding 30,000 passengers per hour.

Defining Optimization Variables

The problem is formulated as a Linear Program to maintain convexity. Let $l \in \{P, G\}$ denote lines and $t \in \{0...23\}$ denote operating hours.

Decision Variables (Continuous)

  • $x_{l,t}$: Frequency of trains dispatched on line $l$ during hour $t$.
  • $y_{l,t}$: Number of passengers successfully served on line $l$ during hour $t$.

Parameters (Constants)

  • $R = 27$: Average revenue per commuter (₹).
  • $C = 22818$: Operational cost per train trip (₹).
  • $K = 2068$: Max crush capacity of a 6-coach train.
  • $F = 58$: Maximum total fleet available.
  • $D_{l,t}$: Expected random passenger demand.

Mathematical Formulation

Objective Function (Expectation Type): Maximize expected daily profit $E[Z]$.

Maximize $E[Z] = \sum_{t=0}^{23} \sum_{l \in \{P,G\}} (27 \cdot y_{l,t} - 22818 \cdot x_{l,t})$

Subject to Constraints:

$y_{l,t} \le 2068 \cdot x_{l,t}$(Physical Capacity)
$y_{l,t} \le D_{l,t}$(Expected Demand Limit)
$x_{P,t} + x_{G,t} \le 58$(Fleet / Rolling Stock Limit)
$x_{l,t} \le 20$(Signaling Safety Upper Bound - 3 min headway)
$x_{l,t} \ge 3$(Minimum Civic Mandate - 20 min headway)

Discussion of Findings

By keeping variables $(x, y)$ continuous, the constraints form a bounded convex polyhedron. Solved via the Simplex algorithm utilizing the lpSolve package in R.

Global Optimum Found

Expected Daily Profit: ₹8,102,022.41

Revenue: ₹15,267,393.00 | Operations Cost: ₹7,165,370.59

  • The solver allocated $\sum x_{P,t} = 174.97$ daily trips to Purple and $\sum x_{G,t} = 139.06$ to Green.
  • Total optimal trips: ~314. This is a massive reduction from the real-world frequency of ~800 daily trips.
  • The algorithm successfully shifted the fleet to target extreme peaks, while dropping strictly to the absolute minimum mandate (3 trains/hr) during dead zones.

Limitations & The "Hidden Constraint"

Limitation: Purely Financial Objective
Because "customer satisfaction" and "wait time" were not quantified as negative cost coefficients in our model, the algorithm actively sacrificed commuter comfort for operational efficiency.

The Hidden Constraint Revealed:

The LP solver ruthlessly eliminated "convenience trips," forcing passengers to wait longer so that every dispatched train operated at its absolute maximum crush capacity (2068 passengers).

This demonstrates mathematically that BMRCL operates with an unquantified hidden constraint—civic convenience. They intentionally operate at a massive financial deficit compared to the mathematical ideal to prevent urban gridlock and maintain commuter sanity.

Conclusion & Future Directions

The optimization model successfully found the mathematical global maximum for transit profitability, highlighting the tension between economics and public utility.

Future Model Iteration: Non-Linear Programming

In future iterations, this model should be advanced to a Non-Linear Program (NLP).

By introducing an exponential "Wait-Time Penalty" coefficient into the objective function (where profit is penalized for every minute a passenger waits over 5 minutes), the model could find the true Pareto-optimal balance between corporate financial sustainability and public passenger satisfaction.
